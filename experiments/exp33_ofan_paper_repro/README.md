# exp33 - reproducing the Ofan paper's simulations

Attempt to reproduce Figs 1, 4, 5, 7, 8 and 12 of "Ofan: Optimal Load Balancing for AI Training" (arXiv 2610.07230) with this repository's htsim (`-strat ofan`, see [`../OFAN_ARCHITECTURE.md`](../OFAN_ARCHITECTURE.md)). Per-figure results: `E1_results.md` (Fig 1), `E4_results.md` (Fig 12), and `E2_results.md` / `E3_results.md` (Figs 7, 8 and 4, 5) when present. Plots are in `plots/` (layout in `plots/README.md`).

This file records where our setup is **not** the paper's, so that differences in the numbers can be read correctly. The topology and the main network parameters match; the items below do not.

## What matches the paper

- 3-tier fat tree, k=12, 432 hosts, 1:1 oversubscription (`topologies/fat_tree_432_1os_3t_800g.topo`; smaller/larger k for Fig 5).
- 800 Gbps links, 0.5 us link latency.
- 800 KB buffer per port and about 80 KB ECN threshold in the congestion-control (CCA) runs (`-q 193`, `-ecn 19 19`).
- About 4 KB payload per packet. 4 MB messages are about 6.4-6.8 RTTs here (BDP about 625-640 KB), the same as the paper's 150-packet RTT.

## Divergences from the paper

1. **Wire format.** Our header is a fixed 64 B and there is no 20 B inter-frame gap or preamble. The paper uses a 62 B header plus the 20 B gap (4,096 B payload). E4 ran at 4,150 B on the wire (no `-mtu`); E1 used `-mtu 4160`. Effect: about 0.4-1% in serialisation time, i.e. the same order as the small CCT differences for Ofan.
2. **Congestion-control-free runs use effectively infinite buffers** (`-q 20000`, `-disable_trim`), matching the paper's footnote 6 (800 KB buffers only in the CCA experiments). The earlier exp32 S5 runs instead used 800 KB buffers without CCA, which is not paper-comparable at message sizes of 16 MB and above (AR/REPS drop packets there).
3. **AR comparator.** Switch AR (`-strat ecmp_ar`) quantises queue sizes relative to the buffer size, so with `-q 20000` every queue looks empty and AR degenerates to random choice. We therefore also ran AR with `-q 193` as a second variant. The paper's AR configuration for the no-CCA runs is not specified in enough detail to know which it corresponds to.
4. **Lower bound.** Our "CCT increase" is relative to an empirical single-flow run with the same configuration (about 17 us at 1 MB, 48.8 us at 4 MB, including the fixed path latency). The paper uses an analytic bound (its Appendix A). The offset matters most for Ofan, whose increase is about 1-3%.
5. **Load pacing (Fig 1).** The paper paces each source at a fraction of line rate. We set the host NIC rate with `-linkspeed 800000*rho` while the switches stay at 800 Gbps. The slope is exact, but `-linkspeed` also feeds RTT/RTO estimates (irrelevant with the constant-window CC), and a constant offset of about 7 us of path latency remains in sender-side CCT.
6. **Utilisation metric (Fig 1, Fig 12).** The simulator has no utilisation time series and the paper does not define its metric. Fig 1 uses "effective utilisation" = size/rate / (CCT - fixed latency); Fig 12 time series are reconstructed from `-log_link` (mean busy fraction of the ToR uplinks, one seed, 1 us bins). Host-link (E->H) utilisation and queues are missing from the reconstruction. REPS/AR utilisation at load 1 comes out 81-83% against the paper's 94-95%; the queue sizes and CCTs agree well, so this is probably the metric, not the simulation (unverified).
7. **DCTCP is a different algorithm.** The repo's DCTCP (`uec.cpp`, `updateCwndOnAck_DCTCP`) only does additive increase and `cwnd -= acked/3` on ECN-marked ACKs; it has no duplicate-ACK or out-of-order reaction. The paper (p. 11) attributes the 309% (REPS) and 205% (AR) DCTCP inflation to its htsim DCTCP's dup-ACK threshold (3 plus the number of paths) being reached by a few flows, which collapse their windows. Our DCTCP inflation is 27% / 17% (REPS / AR). Likely source of the paper's DCTCP: upstream Broadcom/csg-htsim (checked at commit `841d9e7`, March 2026) contains no UEC transport at all; its DCTCP is the TCP-based `sim/dctcp.cpp` (`DCTCPSrc : TcpSrc`), whose base class sets `DUPACK_TH = 3 + rt->size()` (`sim/tcp.cpp:76`, the "3 plus number of paths" of the paper) - but only inside `#ifdef PACKET_SCATTER` (`tcp.h:20` has it commented out, in upstream and here), and `main_tcp.cpp` in both trees never instantiates `DCTCPSrc`. The only DCTCP+scatter drivers are upstream's `datacenter/old_tests/main_dctcp_permutation.cpp` and `main_dctcp_incast_collateral*.cpp` (not in this repo; old topology constructor), which use source-routed scatter, not switch ECMP/AR. So the paper's DCTCP is very probably this TCP-based one (alpha estimator plus dup-ACK threshold), run through a custom driver we do not have, and not the UEC `-sender_cc_algo dctcp` used here. Not tried: porting it (define `PACKET_SCATTER`, adapt the old driver to the current topology and our switch strategies). A cheaper but different test: rerun the UEC DCTCP cells with `-fastlossrecovery` (out-of-order-count triggered recovery, off by default).
8. **NSCC / MSwift parameters.** NSCC and MSwift are identical in every run here (neither shrinks the window), and REPS/AR inflation is 2-4x below the paper's. Initial-window and target-delay parameterisation was not compared with the paper's; not tested.
9. **Missing algorithms.** 4Ofan/POfan (needs ingress-port plumbing), CONGA, DRILL, DLB, SGLB are not implemented. DRB/Ethereal is approximated by `-load_balancing_algo path_rr` (host round robin over all paths, no separate ACK pointer); RPS by `path_random`; RSQ by `-strat ecmp_random`; JSQ by `-strat ecmp_ar -ar_method exact` (exact queue sizes with random tie-break); Simple RR by `-strat ecmp_rr_fixed`.
10. **`-disable_tor_ecn`.** All `-sender_cc_only` runs pass `-disable_tor_ecn` (project rule). Where we checked (REPS in E1, NSCC in E4) omitting it made no measurable difference.
11. **Seeds and sizes.** We use 5-10 seeds (the paper: at least 10 with SEM bars) and, for the 64 MB cells, 1-2 seeds. All-to-all at N=432/1024 is infeasible here (memory grows about N^2), so Fig 5 all-to-all stops at smaller N than the paper's permutation points.
12. **Earlier exp32 runs** used 400 Gbps topologies (16/54-host and the 432-host 400G file), not the paper's 800 Gbps; they validate behaviour but are not figure reproductions.

## What the paper tells us about each figure's configuration

Sources: the paper text and appendix (PDF), and its arXiv TeX source (`https://arxiv.org/src/2610.07230`; extracted copy not stored in the repo). The TeX source contains no code, only `.tex` files and the result PNGs, whose **file names encode the authors' run parameters**, e.g. `fig1_plot5_cct_K12_M256_derangement.png`. Reading of those names: `K` = switch radix, `M` = message size in 4 KB packets, last part = workload. This reading is **inferred**, but it is consistent wherever the text also states the value (K12 = 432 hosts, M256 = 1 MB, M1024 = 4 MB, K64 2-tier = 2,048 hosts, K8 MoE = 128 GPUs, K16 = 1,024 GPUs); the lower bound in Appendix A (17.05694 us "for a message size m = 256") also uses m in packets. Cells marked *(name)* below rest on the file name only.

**Common to all simulations (stated, §6 "Simulator" and "Workloads"):** htsim (Broadcom csg-htsim); default 432-node 3-tier fat tree (k = 12 switches); 800 Gbps links; 0.5 us link latency; 4,096 B payload + 62 B header; 64 B ACKs; 20 B gap between frames (12 B inter-frame gap + 8 B preamble/SFD); per-port buffers 800 KB and ECN threshold 80 KB (10% of the buffer; 10 packets when studying 64 KB packets) **only in the CCA experiments**, otherwise a "practically infinite" buffer (footnote 6); every scenario run at least 10 times with SEM error bars; default permutation message 1 MB; metric CCT = first packet sent until last packet acknowledged, plotted as % increase over a lower bound (analytic for permutations, App. A; "naive" lower bounds for other workloads); with no CCA each source paces at a fixed rate (rho, up to line rate; rho_max under failures) and a host with several flows round-robins them.

**Unknown for all simulations:** the exact AR model (Table 1 lists quanta 5-10-20% but not what the quantisation is relative to when buffers are "infinite"), REPS parameters (Table 1 lists ECN threshold 10%), how the 10+ runs differ (permutation draw only, or also LB random choices), ACK coalescing, ECN marking function (step vs ramp), whether trimming/NACKs are on in the no-CCA runs (the paper mentions no loss recovery there), receiver/NIC model details, DRB/Ethereal/CONGA/DRILL/DLB/SGLB implementations, and the random-number/seed handling. The 4Ofan/pOfan variants use pipes of 4 ports (stated).

## Reading the results

Orderings and trends (Ofan's flat queues and about 1-3% CCT increase; AR/REPS growing queues; Ofan better than AR and REPS for every CCA) reproduce. Absolute numbers that depend on items 1, 4, 6, 7 and 8 above (utilisation, DCTCP inflation, REPS/AR CCA inflation) do not, and should not be compared one-to-one with the paper.
