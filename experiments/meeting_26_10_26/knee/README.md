# knee: what sets the B knee of max FCT in T1/T2, and which parameters move it

Question (user, 2026-10-31): find the parameter or parameter set that controls the FCT knee (smaller or larger on demand). ECN thresholds are excluded (known, uninteresting). Starting point: [`../T3/investigate_8/README.md`](../T3/investigate_8/README.md) closed rate, uniform latency, hosts/paths/tiers, drift, queue swing, token pool and per-EV buffer composition as explanations.

## Answer in six lines (state of 2026-11-01; details and caveats below)
1. The knee is not sharp, and "8" is not exactly right or wrong. With B = 5, 6, 7 added, max FCT decays smoothly from B = 4 to 8 and the knee depends on the rule. Page rule (unpaired, tolerance includes the CI of the tested B): T1 6 / 6 / 5 for r = 0.25 / 0.10 / 0.05 (control r=0.5: 6), T2 6 to 8. Paired rule (per-seed difference against the same seed at B=128; stricter): T1 7 / 7 / 6 (control 6), T2 scope A 6 / 7 / 8 and agg->core 8 / 8 / 8 for 25/75, 10/90, 5/95. So the finer grid puts the knee at 6 to 8, not a different number; moves of one step are inside the noise (3 and 5 seeds). The one clearly resolved step is B = 4 -> 5 (T1 r = 0.05: -7.5 us).
2. Mechanism (correlational from traces, exact token accounting, known-answer cells): a full buffer loses its oldest token; the loss is paid when the buffer later runs empty and the sender draws a path uniformly at random; one overwrite costs 1.0 to 2 draws (marked or trimmed replies kill tokens, the next send draws again). A lost token costs only if the walk later drains: where the B=128 buffer ends the run holding a surplus (`lat_m20000`: about 60 EVs), clipped tokens were never needed and overwrites are free (exact identity: draws = sends - clean ACKs + overwrites + final level, 0 residual).
3. What makes a random draw expensive is **critical load**, and unevenness scales it. Same denominator (max-FCT excess per extra random draw summed over the traced hosts): T2 healthy fabric, load 0.5: about 1e-6 us per draw (34% random draws at B=1, FCT unchanged, 704.65 vs 704.62); T1 uniform r=0.5, load 1.0: 0.0003 to 0.001 us; T1 r=0.05, load 1.0: 0.005 to 0.009 us. The uniform control has a real knee too (708.1 -> 702.7 us from B=5 to 6). So "a random draw may hit a bad path" is true (cost per draw rises 6 to 10x with unevenness) but is not the whole story and not required. Your intuition (replacing a good EV costs more) holds in effect; the evicted EV itself is never special (degraded as often as the incoming one).
4. Dials without touching REPS or the ECN thresholds. **Up (big): repeated messages on the same src-dst pair** (a workload parameter plus the BDP): for the second and later messages the REPS buffer carried over from the previous message is the issue, and the knee moves from 8 (fresh buffer, as in the long flows) to about the number of packets in flight at the message boundary: B_fct (paired, max-FCT of ToR0 messages 2-4, 8 seeds) = 64 at the base fabric (200G, RTT 8.3 us, 4150 B), 96 with link latency x2, 128 with MTU 2075, 32 at half the send rate; mechanism proven from traces (the B=256 buffer carries 54 / 88 / 102 EVs into message 2 against 60 / 110 / 113 packets in flight; message 2's first 300 sends contain 4 random draws at B=256 and 83 to 148 at B=8). The knee scales with rate x RTT / MTU, so latency, rate and packet size are the dials, and the gap between messages (60 or 100 us) is not. Needs `[FIX: connection-info-cwnd-overflow]` to run with the constant-rate sender. On the 16 MiB T1/T2 flows repeated twice the start-up cost is only 0.5 to 1.5% of the flow, so the message-2 knee is 48 to 96 at a 0.2% tolerance, 8 to 32 at 1%, and 4 to 8 at 2 to 5% (message length matters: for 1 MiB messages the knee holds up to 5% tolerance). **Down: load slack** (rate below the degraded capacity): 128-host 2-tier 6 -> 3 at load 0.7; T1 1024 hosts 6 -> 4; T2 6 -> 5 (one to two steps, below-knee cost cut 2 to 5x). **No effect or not robust:** MTU 1500 on long flows (one step, and 28% of its cells enter Freezing Mode), latency spread, oscillating incast delay (stretches the overwrite tail 4 to 8x, not the FCT knee), send rate on long flows, degraded fraction, hosts, paths, tiers, uniform latency. ECN thresholds / BDP-scaled latency (excluded) also move it.
5. Why long flows have no big dial but repeated messages do: on a long flow the token walk drains back near zero, overwrites(B) is a dimensionless cliff that does not move under rescaling, and the cost per random draw is already at its maximum at matched load. On a repeated message the buffer carries tokens that the next message cannot use for one RTT (no ACKs return), so the first RTT sends min(B, in-flight) recycled EVs and the rest uniformly random: the cost is (in-flight - B) draws per message, which is large relative to a 1 MiB message (5 to 20% FCT at small B) and vanishes exactly when B reaches the in-flight count.
6. Random draws against max FCT (T1/T2 pages): the random-draw share keeps falling slightly after max FCT is inside the noise (about one step later in T1, up to 8 steps in the agg->core scope of T2, tolerance-dependent); the cost model fitted on B <= 2 predicts the knee within one step in 3 of 4 T1 configs and 2 of 6 T2 configs; correlations pooled over B are inflated (B drives both), paired B >= 4 correlations are 0.83 to 0.91.

## Hypothesis (from the code)
- A full buffer overwrites its oldest valid slot (`buffer_reps.cpp:25`): one token is lost, the path mix is not changed (investigate_8 Analysis 5).
- The loss is paid only when the buffer is later empty: `nextEntropy_freezing` (`uec.cpp:4138-4168`) draws `rand() % _no_of_paths`, uniform over all paths, so about half of those draws land on a degraded path whatever r is, while REPS otherwise puts only about r of its sends there.
- So the cost of a too-small B is (extra random draws) x (share landing on degraded paths) x (extra delay of a degraded packet), and it grows as r falls, which matches the T1 B=1 minus B=8 excess (12, 97, 136 us for r = 0.25, 0.10, 0.05).

## Direction change (user, 2026-10-31): the dial must be a network parameter, not a REPS change
The earlier draw-bias flag (`-reps_draw_bad_evs`, `-reps_draw_bad_q`) was never validated and has been replaced by `-reps_draw_weights` (see `experiments/REPS_DRAW_WEIGHTS_ARCHITECTURE.md`, results in the god-mode sections below); it changes REPS, so it is a causal test of the cost reading, not a dial. Dials must be link / topology / latency / buffer / switch / workload parameters. Phase A gives the structure: FCT excess(B) = overwrites(B) x (1.5 to 2 extra draws) x 0.5 x cost per degraded draw (rises as r falls; B=4 value under-predicts B=8 by about 2x), and overwrites(B) falls off a cliff (more than 10x per step of 2 in B). So there are two kinds of dial: **amplitude** (cost per draw: depth r, fraction f, degraded-link service time, switch queue depth on the degraded link) which moves the knee by about one step per 10x, and **location** (where overwrites(B) ends: the tail of the in-flight swing in packets), which can move it a lot.

### Remaining network dials (predictions written before the runs)
The cost model says FCT excess is the number of extra degraded landings times their cost, and the cost exists because the degraded links are exactly critical (load = capacity). Two network parameters change the cost without touching REPS or ECN:
- **D3 slack**: send rate R below the matched value with the degrade file fixed (mean ratio 0.5): ToR0 uplink load R/200. Slack lets the degraded links absorb misrouted packets, so the cost per landing falls. Prediction: knee falls monotonically as R falls: B_fct 6 at 200, 6 at 190, 4 at 180 (T3 F cells agree), 2 to 3 at 160, 1 to 2 at 140. Not a dial upwards (R > 200 overloads).
- **D4 fraction of degraded uplinks f** (of 8), degraded depth 0.05, healthy uplinks at (4 - 0.05 f) / (8 - f) so that ToR0 capacity stays 50% (f = 1, 2, 4: healthy ratio 0.564, 0.65, 0.95): a uniform random draw lands on a degraded uplink with probability f/8, so the excess at small B scales with f. Prediction: B_fct 6 at f=4 (the baseline), 4 at f=2, 2 to 4 at f=1.

Mechanism, "consistent with" (not established by an intervention): FCT excess(B) = overwrites(B) x about 1.5 to 2 extra random draws per overwrite (a draw landing on a degraded path comes back ECN-marked in 64 to 99% of cases, so the token is killed and the next send draws again: 1 / (1 - q p_kill)) x the share q = 0.5 of uniform draws that land on a degraded path x the cost of one forced degraded landing. The cost exists because the degraded links are exactly critical at the matched load (slack removes it: D3). Overwrites(B) is a dimensionless curve with a cliff (more than 10x per two steps of B), identical under rescaling of packet size and rate (D1, D2). Therefore the FCT knee (a 0.2% threshold read from that cliff) is pinned near 6 and moves by about one grid step per 10x of amplitude; a large upward move needs the overwrite curve itself to be stretched while the cost per landing stays large, which only raised standing queues (higher ECN thresholds, BDP-scaled latency) achieve in the data on file, and which a spread of path latencies achieves for the overwrite proxy but not for FCT.
Not tested / limits: upward dial not found among link / topology parameters except the excluded ECN-threshold family; 128-host topology for D1 to D5 (T3 says hosts do not move the knee, but the D-series was not repeated on 1024 hosts or T2's 3-tier); 3 seeds (D5 +10..+20 8 seeds); the draw-bias causal test (oracle draw, a REPS change) was allowed by the user as a check but not as a dial; I reverted it after building it and before validating it, so the cost reading rests on A1-A3 and the known-answer cells (no intervention on the draw); `lat_m20000` conversion collapse unexplained; Test K's "1.5% overwrites cost nothing" in `../T3/investigate_8/README.md` still stands uncorrected (needs a note: that cell is a mark-flood regime where overwrites do not become draws).

### Corrections after the B=7 / r=0.5 / max-FCT-host work (2026-11-01)
1. **Knee rule as implemented on the T1/T2 pages (`plot_common.knee_fct`)**: B_fct = smallest B such that every larger B has mean max FCT at most the B=128 mean plus a tolerance (or lower); tolerance = the largest of the 95% t-CI half-width of that B, the CI half-width of B=128, and 0.2% of the B=128 mean; one-sided, unpaired. I had described it to the user as "the larger of the CI of B=128 and 0.2%"; the CI of the tested B is part of it too. This README's own analyses (D-series, slack) use the paired variant (per-seed difference against the reference, 1.96 SE of the differences, or 0.2%): both rules are given where used.
2. **The uniform control weakens "unevenness is what makes random draws costly".** T1 r=0.5 (uniform, no degraded/healthy contrast) has a real step in max FCT from B=5 to 6 (708.1 -> 702.7 us, CIs 3.5 and 0.5) with the random share falling 18.6% -> 2.1%, and B_fct = 6 (older M4 data: 26% random draws at B=4, +6.7 us). So random draws cost FCT also without a degraded path to land on. My Phase A regression could not tell the two apart (the degraded share of uniform random draws is fixed at 0.5). Per extra random draw the uniform cost is smaller (about 0.0008 us at B=4 against about 0.005 us at r=0.05, 6x), so unevenness raises the cost per draw but is not required for it; the part of the story "a lost token costs because the replacement draw may hit a bad path" is true but incomplete: at the matched (critical) load any departure of the spray from the recycled, load-balanced pattern costs, and a random draw is such a departure. The oracle draw (REPS change, reverted) would separate the two.

### Closing summary of the "small or big on demand" question
- **Long single flow per pair (T1/T2/T3 workload, one flow per pair):** floor of 6 to 8 (paired 6 to 8; 5 to 7 on the page rule). Down: load slack, 1 to 2 grid steps. Up: nothing robust (only higher ECN thresholds / BDP-scaled latency, excluded; MTU 1500 one step on gate-clear seeds; spread, incast, rate, fraction, hosts, paths, tiers, uniform latency: none).
- **Repeated messages on the same pair (a workload change; needs the cwnd fix):** the knee follows the in-flight window, about rate x RTT / MTU packets (64 base, 96 at latency x2, 128 at MTU 2075, 32 at half rate, for 1 MiB messages; mechanism from traces): a dial in both directions made of network parameters (RTT, rate, packet size) once the workload has repeated pairs. - **Honest limits:** one fabric size per variant (128 hosts for the dial scan; T1 1024 hosts and T2 3-tier only for the 16 MiB check), constant-rate sender only, matched load, one message shape; the "big" dial is therefore a statement about repeated messages, not about the long-flow knee the T1/T2 pages show.

### Decision (user, 2026-11-01): option 1 accepted, and a design for the next dial
**Decision.** The upward dial is **repeated messages on the same src-dst pair** (a workload change on top of network parameters): knee about the packets in flight (rate x RTT / MTU): 32 to 128 for 1 MiB messages; on the 16 MiB T1/T2 flows it is tolerance-dependent (message 2: 96 / 48 at 0.2%, 32 / 8 at 1%, 4 to 8 at 2 to 5% on T2 / T1). Downward dial: load slack. The long single flow keeps a floor of 6 to 8.
**Next dial (user idea): a degradation that changes with time**, e.g. the degraded and healthy halves of the ToR0 uplinks swap (r = x on the even links becomes 1 - x, and back) every period T, or the distribution itself changes. Reading: the buffer then holds EVs that were good before the swap and are bad after it, so a larger B holds more stale information and may be harmful (the knee could become an optimum, B too large hurts), the opposite of every cell so far where a larger B never hurt.
Feasibility (checked in the source, nothing built): a runtime hook exists, `BaseQueue::update_bit_rate(linkspeed_bps)` (`queue.h:70`), so a new EventSource can change the service rate of chosen ToR uplink queues at times t_k (the `-degrade_links_file` machinery fixes rates at construction only; `LinkFailureEvent` in `main_uec.cpp` is the pattern for a timed event). Predictions (to be written again before any run): 1. #### Cost estimator test (written before the run)

**God mode (item 4), predictions written before any god-mode run.** Mechanism `[ADDED: reps-draw-weights]` (`-reps_draw_weights capacity|w0,..,w(P-1)`, `-reps_draw_hosts`, `-reps_draw_scope`, `-reps_draw_invert`): the uniform `rand() % _no_of_paths` of the random branch of `nextEntropy_freezing` becomes one weighted draw, weight of EV e = bottleneck speed of the route of path e % P (20 vs 380 Gbit/s here, so a draw lands on a 5% link with probability 4 x 20 / (4 x 20 + 4 x 380) = 5% instead of 50%).
