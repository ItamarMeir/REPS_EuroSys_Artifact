# investigate_8: what sets the k tail (and so the knee) when the ECN thresholds are well set

Question (user, 2026-10-30): the buffer must hold the train of consecutive clean ACKs; with sensible thresholds, what sets the size of that train?
Plan: analysis of traces already on disk first (B=128 uncapped traces of T1, T2, T3, steady window = drop first and last 15% of each traced source's span), then at most one or two interventions.

## Predictions (written before the analysis)
1. Mean clean-ACK run length between marked ACKs is about 1/p, i.e. 15 to 30 at 3 to 6% marks, well above 8. So run length alone cannot set B = 8; what matters is the excess of clean arrivals over sends inside a run (bunching).
2. The k tail follows the long-horizon spread of (clean ACKs - sends) (and the RTT spread of clean ACKs) better than it follows the mark rate p.
3. RTT spread of clean ACKs orders the shapes like the k tail: T2 25/75 > 10/90 > 5/95 > uniform.
Misses are reported below.

## Analysis 1 (traces on disk; `ack_trains.py`, `drift.py`; data `data/ack_trains.csv`, `data/ack_trains_seeds.csv`; 21 cells, T1 x3, T2 x8 + healthy, T3 x11, 3 to 5 seeds, steady window, traced hosts)
Identity first: with c = valid EVs in the buffer, c rises by 1 per clean ACK and falls by 1 per send (floored at 0), so c = M - running min of M, where M(t) = clean ACKs - sends. The replay of c matches the observed k per cell (cell-mean Pearson 0.99; mean c 11.0 against k 12.7 on T2 25/75, 3.7 against 4.4 on uniform; the replay is about 0.6 to 1.7 lower, k counts the EV valid before the draw). So k is the height of a reflected walk, nothing else.

Predictions vs outcome:
1. Clean-ACK run length about 1/p, well above 8: **held, and larger**: mean run 23 to 620 ACKs (steady window), p99 run 43 to 780. A train of 8 is short next to a run. Run length does not predict k either (Pearson -0.22 against k mean). So "B holds the train" is not the right picture: the buffer never sees the whole run because every send pops one EV.
2. k follows the long-horizon spread of (clean ACKs - sends) or the RTT spread better than p: **missed**. Std of the change of M over 20 and 100 us (Pearson -0.30, -0.29) and the clean-ACK RTT spread (Pearson 0.00) do not predict k mean; T2 25/75 (k 12.7) and 5/95 (k 5.3) have M spread 1.6 vs 1.8 (20 us). The mark share p has Spearman -0.67 with k mean (more marks, smaller k), but within T2 the shape with the most marks (25/75) has the largest k, so p does not collapse the cells either.
3. RTT spread orders the shapes like k: **missed** (spread 2.00, 1.62, 1.81, 1.24 us for 25/75, 10/90, 5/95, uniform; k 12.7, 7.3, 5.3, 4.4).

What does predict k: the **net drift of M** (slope over the steady window, pkt/us). Spearman 0.92 against k mean over the 21 cells (Pearson 0.55, a few cells with large negative slope, e.g. base slope -0.12, q394_ecn25 -0.25, have k 2.6 to 3). More negative drift means a lower k. T2 25/75 has a positive slope (+0.013 to +0.016), i.e. clean ACKs slightly exceed sends over the window, and the largest k (11 to 12.7); u50 has slope -0.004 to -0.016 and k 4.4 to 4.6.
Reading (consistent with, not shown by intervention): the walk has almost zero drift in many cells (clean ACKs per send 0.998 to 1.003), so its excursion is set by the drift, which is the fraction of sends whose ACK returns no clean EV (marks, trims) minus any positive in-flight change, and REPS itself drives that fraction to a few percent or less by moving load off the degraded paths. A lower mark share means a larger k, which is the opposite of the "ECN thresholds too low" direction and explains why the 25/75 shape, where REPS can almost fully avoid marks, has the largest tail.
Caveats: drift is computed from the same events as k, so a correlation is partly mechanical (M is the walk); pooled over cells with different topologies; 21 points; no intervention yet.

## Analysis 3: in-flight drawdown (`inflight_range.py`, data `data/inflight.csv`; 25 cells, steady window, traced hosts, column `inflight_pkts` at each send)
Token argument (exact for the walk, not a new measurement): each EV is a token. A clean ACK returns a token to the buffer, a send takes one, and a send with an empty buffer creates a new random token. So the token count N only ratchets up to the largest in-flight count seen, and is reduced by marked ACKs; the buffer holds c = N - in-flight, i.e. the drawdown of the in-flight count from its running maximum. In-flight is rate x mean RTT of the packets in flight (200G, 4150 B packets: 6 packets per us). So k is the amplitude of the in-flight (queueing delay) wander measured downward from its recent peak, and a buffer of B holds it when B exceeds that amplitude.
Data: the drawdown of the in-flight count from its running maximum (`dd_mean`) has Pearson 0.84 with k mean over the 25 cells (Spearman 0.65; the mean drawdown is 0.5 to 1.7 packets below k mean, as in investigate_k, k counts the EV before the draw). The in-flight standard deviation is 1.0 to 3.3 packets (0.2 to 0.5 us of mean RTT) and, inside T2, orders like k (uniform 1.1, 5/95 1.45, 10/90 1.45, 25/75 2.75 pkt; k mean 4.4, 5.3, 7.3, 12.7) and the threshold cells (q394_ecn98 2.16 vs ecn25 1.95, k 6.3 vs 2.6 only partly). Across all cells the in-flight std / range correlate only weakly (Pearson 0.46 / 0.48, Spearman 0.08 / 0.09): the running-max drawdown matters, not the spread alone, and mark rate sets how fast tokens are removed.
Reading (consistent with, not shown by an intervention): the knee B = 8 is the in-flight swing, in packets, of the path queueing delay at well-configured thresholds: about 1 us of mean-RTT swing at 200G is 6 packets. What can move it is anything that changes that swing or how fast marks cut the token pool: the ECN thresholds (queue allowed to wander higher), and the shape (25/75 leaves near-critical, almost unmarked links with 100G capacity carrying 24% of the ACKs, so the queue there wanders; at 5/95 the degraded links are abandoned and marked). Missing: a direct measurement of queue occupancy over time on the near-critical links (the sim logs only the max queue per link, so this needs a queue-timeseries log) and a run that changes the swing without changing the marks.

## Answer so far to "what moves the knee, and why" (all statements are 'consistent with'; one natural experiment, no clean single-variable proof)
- The buffer holds tokens: c = (token pool) - (in-flight). The pool ratchets up to the largest in-flight count seen and is pruned by every mark or trim. So k (and the knee) is the in-flight swing in packets, cut down by the rate of token-killing events.
- Moves it: (a) fewer kill events: higher ECN thresholds, lower send rate (fewer marks), a larger share of traffic left near-critical but unmarked on degraded links (r = 0.10 to 0.45, B_ow 12); (b) a mark flood does the reverse (r = 0.20: k 1.8). (c) Larger in-flight swing, e.g. 4x latency with scaled queues and thresholds.
- Does not move it (T3): hosts, paths, tiers, link latency at fixed thresholds. Max FCT is flat from B = 4 to 6 in every clean cell because the extra overwrites at B < B_ow cost only 0.1 to 1% of FCT, so the FCT knee is below the overwrite knee.
- Limits: kill events and the in-flight swing are measured per run, not per host and window; the r = 0.20 regime change is not explained (why 80G slow links produce ten times the marks); no run changes the in-flight swing without changing marks; queue-occupancy time series is not logged.

## Analysis 4: token-pool identity check (`token_pool.py`, seed 42, hosts 0-7, steady window, B=128)
Test of the token argument: N = valid EVs (`fresh` at the send) + `inflight_pkts`, read at consecutive sends, should rise only at a send with a random EV and fall only after a marked ACK or NACK. Result: **partly supported**. Every +1 explained by a random draw is there (389 of 401 random sends step N up in `base_h1024_p32`; 9 of 9 in `r025_R200`) and kills step N down (344 of 378; 8 of 8). But N also steps by +-1 at many other sends (3579 up / 3148 down steps in `base`, 1260 / 1240 in `r025_R200`, against 401 and 9 random sends), so N is not conserved between kill and random events. The balanced extra steps are consistent with one ACK covering two packets while returning one EV (SACK coalescing) or with the logging order of `fresh` and `inflight_pkts` within a timestamp; I did not isolate which. So the ratchet picture holds for the random and kill events, but N has a bounded +-1 jitter (range 36 to 71 over the window, against a mean of about 510), which the argument does not capture. The exact identity "k = drawdown of N from its running max" is therefore not demonstrated; only the weaker investigate_k statement (k approximately the drawdown of in-flight, Pearson 0.84 across cells) is.

## Test J: path-latency spread (new knob `latency_tor_agg`, predictions written before the run, 2026-10-30)
New mechanism `per-link-extra-latency` (extra one-way delay on selected ToR->agg pipes, `-degrade_links_file` line `latency_tor_agg <tor> <agg> <ns>`; speed, queue, ECN untouched). Validation (`validate_extra_lat.py`, 128-host cell, B=8, seed 42): flag absent, old vs new binary, 128 flow-finish lines identical; 0 ns lines identical to none; known answer +10000 ns on all 128 ToR->agg uplinks gives p50 FCT 700.29 -> 720.07 us (+19.78 us, predicted +20.0: each data packet and each ACK crosses one such pipe); out-of-range tor/agg, negative ns and a missing field exit with an error and simulate nothing.
Question: does widening the spread of ACK return times (which no mark or shape change) raise k and the knee? Cells (1024 hosts, 200G, ECN 25/76, B in {4,6,8,12,16,128}, seeds 42-44; 72 cells): `lat_u2000` control: +2 us on every ToR0 uplink (shifts all RTTs, no spread); `lat_m2000`, `lat_m5000`: +2 / +5 us on half of ToR0's uplinks (agg % 4 in {2,3}, balanced over degraded/healthy parity) at 5/95; `lat_m5000_r025`: +5 us on half at 25/75. References: `base_h1024_p32` (5/95) and `r025_R200` (25/75), same seeds.
Predictions (RTT spread across paths and in-flight swing are rate x spread, 6 packets per us at 200G; marks should stay about the same, which I check):
1. Control `lat_u2000`: k and knee unchanged against `base_h1024_p32` (k mean about 2.9, p99 6, B_ow 8); a uniform shift of RTT is not a lever (as the latency axis at fixed thresholds already showed).
2. Spread: `lat_m2000` k p99 at least 8 (B_ow 12), `lat_m5000` k p99 at least 12 (B_ow 12 to 16); the increase scales with the spread.
3. `lat_m5000_r025`: k p99 above the 25/75 reference (12).
4. Marks per run (ECN ACKs + trims at B=8) within 2x of the reference in every J cell, so the change is not a threshold/marking effect. If k does not rise with the spread, ACK-arrival spread is not what sets the tail and that reading is withdrawn.

That is a controlled demonstration, with the uniform shift as the control, of the user's intuition: spread in ACK arrivals sets how often a valid EV is overwritten. It acts on the tail of the backlog (overwrites, B_ow) and hardly on its mean (k mean +0.4); the 128-slot buffer only matters when the tail exceeds B.
But the **max FCT knee does not move**: B_fct is 6 in all four 5/95 cells (base, control and both spread cells; max FCT at B = 8 is within 0.4% of B = 128: 722.5 vs 723.4 us at +5 us) because the overwrites it adds (0.1%) cost nothing measurable in FCT (Test K below shows that even 1.5% do not). The max FCT knee therefore moved only in the q394 pair and in the lat4_bdpq cell (where overwrites exceed 0.9% at B = 8). Limits: 3 seeds, one topology (1024 hosts, 32 paths), latency added on ToR0's uplinks only (and on the ACKs of flows into ToR0), the spread is a two-level step not a continuous jitter; the 25/75 spread cell is in a different regime and uninformative.

## Test K: does a large enough spread move the max FCT knee? (predictions written before the run, 2026-10-30)
Test J moved the overwrite knee but not the max FCT knee, because 0.1% overwrites cost nothing. Hypothesis: the max FCT knee moves when the overwrites at B = 8 become large enough to cost FCT, about 1% or more (`lat4_bdpq` has 0.93% at B = 8 and knee 12; the q394_ecn98 cell 0.2%?). Intervention: larger spread, same shape, thresholds and rate: `lat_m20000`, `lat_m50000` (+20 / +50 us on uplinks with agg % 4 in {2,3} of ToR0), 5/95, B in {4, 8, 12, 16, 32, 128}, seeds 42-44, 36 cells.
Predictions: (1) overwrites at B = 8 rise with the spread (above 0.5% at +20 us, above 1% at +50 us) and B_ow moves to 16 or 32; (2) B_fct moves up to 12 or more in the cell(s) where overwrites at B = 8 exceed about 1%, and max FCT at B = 8 is at least 1% above B = 128 there; (3) marks per run stay within 2x of the base (3805), otherwise the cell is in another regime (as `lat_m5000_r025`) and is not read as a spread effect. A knee that stays at 6 with 3% overwrites at B = 8 would falsify the cost argument.

## Analysis 8: a more sensitive FCT metric (predictions written before the run)
The max over 1024 flows is a single noisy sample; the slowest flow is always one of the 32 ToR0 sources. Metric: mean FCT of the 32 ToR0 sources (every B, from `traces/*.stdout.txt`, 3 seeds, one-sided knee rule of `aggregate_t3.py`: smallest B from which every larger B is within max(CI, 0.2%) of B = 128 or better). Prediction: this metric's knee follows the overwrite knee, i.e. 8 or less in `base_h1024_p32` and `lat_u2000`, 12 in `r025_R200`, `lat_m2000`, `lat_m5000`; if the knees of the mean stay at 4 to 6 in every cell, the spread and shape effect is too small to register in FCT at all.
Outcome (`tor0_mean_knee.py`, `data/tor0_mean_knee.csv`): **missed**. The knee of the mean FCT of the 32 ToR0 sources is 6 in the base, in `lat_u2000`, `lat_m2000`, `lat_m5000`, `r010_R200`, `r015_R200` and `r025_R200`, and 4 in `lat_m20000`, `r035_R200` and `r045_R200`; it does not follow the overwrite knee (8 to 12 in the spread and 25/75 cells). The mean is B-dependent only below B = 6 (base: 751.5 us at B=2, 717.8 at B=4, 710.1 at B=6, 710.4 at B=128; +1.0% at B=4). So even a metric with 30x less noise than the max over flows shows an FCT knee at 6 that spread and shape do not move: at B >= 6 the extra overwrites those levers cause (0.1 to 1.5% at B=8) are not visible in FCT. This closes the FCT side: the FCT knee (max or mean) sits at 4 to 6 wherever the thresholds are the baseline ones, and the spread and shape levers show up only in overwrites and k.
