# knee_16paths: the N sweep on a 16-path fabric

The same experiment as `../knee/` (N sweep: the link delay is the only parameter, N = packets in flight 24..790, REPS B ladder, god mode, speed of light, uplink-class and EV-distribution analyses), run on a fabric with 16 paths instead of 8. Interactive page: `plots/nsweep.html` (`scripts/build_page_nsweep.py`). Nothing here modifies `../knee/`; scripts are copies, parametrised by `scripts/params.py`.

## Fabric and what is the same
- 2-tier fat tree, k = 32 switches: **16 hosts per ToR, 16 uplinks of 400G per ToR, 16 spines, 32 ToRs, 512 hosts**. Topology files `data/topo/topo_512_d16_lat<ns>.topo` (same format as the 8-path ones: `Radix_Down 16, Radix_Up 16`, tier 1 `Radix_Down 32`); no simulator change was needed (`fat_tree_topology.cpp:505-545` is satisfied: 512 ToR uplinks, 16 aggregation switches per pod). The printed `no_of_tor_uplinks: 512` confirms it.
- **SRv6** (`-use_srv6 -paths 16`, printed line "SRv6 source routing enabled"): EV -> `_paths[ev % 16]`, path j = ToR0 uplink j. ToR0 uplinks are 5/95 (`data/degrade/degrade_h512_p16.txt`: even agg 0.05 = 20G, odd 0.95 = 380G, mean 0.5): 8 slow and 8 fast links, so a uniform draw lands on a 5% link with probability 50%, a capacity-weighted one with 8 x 20 / (8 x 20 + 8 x 380) = 5%.
- Same per host: 200G constant-rate sender (ToR0 offered 16 x 200G = 3200G = degraded capacity, matched), MTU 4150, 4 messages of 4 MiB, 1000 us apart (`-connections_mapping`), tornado host i -> i + 256, queues 101 packets, ECN 25/76, delay per link 0.25 .. 16 us (same RTT 2.337 .. 128.337 us as printed by `network_max_unloaded_rtt`), REPS = `-load_balancing_algo freezing`, `-disable_tor_ecn`, trimming on, seeds 42-49. B ladder = the 8-path ladder plus B = 2, 4, 8, 16 for both arms (`data/bsmap_knee16.json`, 91 values per seed and arm).
- Different: 2048 flows per cell (4x), 16 degraded hosts (all behind ToR0) instead of 8, 16 EVs instead of 8. Only ToR0 is degraded: 16 of 512 hosts (3%) instead of 8 of 128 (6%).
- Metric: max FCT over the 16 degraded hosts 0-15 (stdout), mean of messages 2-4 per seed. The "max-FCT host" and "best-FCT host" of the class and EV plots are the max / min FCT among hosts 0-15, all of which are traced.

### N against the topology and the network (checked 2026-11-08)
N is a measurement (peak `inflight_pkts` of host 0 in message 1), not a formula. It is the unloaded bandwidth-delay product plus 11 to 15 packets:
- Unloaded RTT = 8 x link delay + 0.337 us. A path has 4 links each way. 0.337 us = 4 x (4150 B at 400G = 83 ns) + 4 x (64 B ACK at 400G = 1.3 ns); the simulator prints `network_max_unloaded_rtt 2.33712` at 0.25 us per link.
- Send gap of the 200G constant-rate sender = 4150 B x 8 / 200G = 166 ns (matches the trace timestamps).
- Unloaded BDP = RTT / gap = 14.1, 26.1, 50.2, 98.4, 194.8, 387.6, 773.1 packets for 0.25 ... 16 us per link.
- Warmup (sends before the first valid EV send, median) = 17, 29, 53, 100, 196, 387, 770, that is 1.21 ... 0.996 x BDP. The first ACK returns 0.40 us after the unloaded RTT at every latency (8 x delay + 0.74 us measured), which is 2.4 sends and explains why warmup is above the BDP at small N.
- N = 25, 39, 65, 113, 208, 400, 788 = BDP + 10.9, 12.9, 14.8, 14.6, 13.2, 12.4, 14.9. The peak is reached late in the flow (lat 250, B 32, seed 42: send 635 at 108 us), when ACKs of packets queued on the 20G links are delayed, so it is not the warmup BDP. From 8 to 16 us N grows by 388 packets = 48.5 per us against 48.2 analytic (8 links x 200G / 33200 bit), so N scales exactly with the path delay.
- The god-mode views use the N labels and the warmup table of the REPS traces (`n_mech.py`, `n_warmup.py` ran on the REPS arm only).
- Section 6 of the page shows N, the unloaded BDP, N - BDP and warmup / BDP (the earlier column warmup / N, 0.68 ... 0.98, was removed: it mixed the two definitions).

## Queue-depth control (2026-11-08): do trims and retransmissions cause the growth of the gap with N?
Evidence so far (no intervention, `data/n_um_*.csv`): the max-FCT host of messages 2-4 finishes about 5 us + 1 RTT above the speed of light whenever it had a post-warmup NACK, and about 5 us above when it had none, at every N and in both arms. Split by the class of the trimmed packet (corrected attribution, see below): REPS max hosts at N >= 208 have NACKs only on the 5% links (234 of 384 at N = 788) or on both classes (150), median excess 1.27 RTT and 0.99 RTT; god-arm hosts with NACKs only on the 95% links (10 at N = 788) have 0.97 RTT, hosts with both 1.04 RTT.

**Test.** Same cells with `-q 404` and `-q 808` (4x and 8x the 101-packet queue), `-ecn 25 76` unchanged. `alloc_queue` scales queue size and ECN thresholds of a degraded link by its ratio, so the 95% links hold 384 / 768 packets, the 5% links 20 / 40 packets (not only the 95% links: raising only the 95% queues needs a simulator change), healthy links 404 / 808. N = 400 and 788 (lat 8000, 16000), B = 32 and 256, REPS and god arm, 2 seeds (42, 43; a quick test, so differences are read against the spread of the two, not a confidence interval), paired by seed against the q = 101 cells of the main sweep; new config names `h512_p16_msgNh[G]q<q>_lat<L>` (`scripts/make_knee16_q_configs.py`, `data/configs_knee16_q.json`, `data/bsmap_knee16_q.json`). 32 cells.

**Predictions (written before the run).**
1. God arm, N = 788, q = 808: trims on the 95% uplinks fall to about 0 (the 768-packet queue is as large as the packets one host has in flight). The max-host excess then falls from about 130 us to the standing queue delay of the 95% links (a full 768-packet queue is 67 us at 380G) plus about 5 us; it should be well below one RTT (128 us). Refuted if the 95% trims are about 0 and the median max-host excess stays above 0.7 RTT.
2. REPS arm, N = 788: trims on the 5% links remain (queue 20 / 40 packets, 8 slow links, 36-46% random draws), so the excess falls less than in the god arm; if it falls as much, the 5% trims are not what costs the RTT.
3. N = 400 behaves like N = 788 scaled by RTT (64 us).
4. ECN marking on the 95% links (39-47% of ACKs in the god arm at large B) is not predicted to change with q, since the thresholds do not; if it vanishes, the marks come from queue depth reaching the thresholds, not from the draws.
5. 5% links: a 40-packet queue holds up to 66 us of delay; if the god-arm FCT gets worse than at q = 101, that delay replaces the trim cost.

### Correction (2026-11-08): class attribution of ACKs and NACKs in `n_uplink_mech.py`
The first version credited every answer (ACK, NACK) to the class of the LATEST transmission of the same seqno. A NACK that arrives after a retransmission of that seqno was therefore counted for the retransmission's class: in one lat 16000 cell 5771 of 17384 NACKs (33%) had been retransmitted before the NACK arrived, and the old numbers showed hundreds of NACKs on the 95% paths while the trace has none (NACKs by their own EV: only even paths, equal to the ToR0 uplink trims). The answer row carries the EV of the packet it answers (per flow and EV, answers == transmissions, 1024 of 1024 pairs), so answers are now attributed by (seqno, EV). Effect, whole study re-extracted (`data/um_before_fix/` keeps the old files): send columns identical; ECN counts and ECN shares by class identical; trims by class changed: REPS trim rate of 95% links at B >= 32 was 0.7 / 2.8 / 8.3% at N = 208 / 400 / 788 and is now 0.0 / 0.5 / 0.9%, of 5% links 58 / 75 / 79% now 60 / 78 / 85%; god arm 95% 6.8 / 7.3 / 6.5% now 6.8 / 7.3 / 6.2%, 5% links 15 / 15 / 9% now 16 / 15 / 16%. The page's trim-rate-by-class plot is rebuilt. The god-mode ECN anomaly is NOT an artifact of this bug: its numbers did not change (95% links 43-50% marked from N = 113, 5% links the same, both classes equal to within 1-3 points; where the marks are set is not known, the trace only records that the ACK carries a mark).

## Buffer state after each message (2026-11-08): what does the REPS buffer hold when a message has finished?
Plot in section 3 of the page (right after the max-FCT plot), from `scripts/n_bufstate.py` (last event row of every traced flow: `slots` after the final ACK; hosts 0-15 x messages 1-4, both arms; `data/n_bufstate_reps.csv`, `n_bufstate_god.csv`). Valid slots only, path = EV % 16.
Predictions (written before the extraction): (1) B >= N: the buffer is nearly full (valid / B close to 1) after each message and almost all valid entries are on the 95% (odd) paths; the 5% paths hold little because their packets are marked or trimmed and their EVs are not recycled. (2) B << N (B 32 at N = 788): valid entries stay at most B, with a small non-zero share on the 5% paths. (3) N = 788, large B: valid / B clearly below 1 after messages 2-4, because trims invalidate entries and the stock is not refilled in time; this is the quantitative test of "the stock covers a smaller share of the pipe as N grows". (4) God arm: the share of valid entries on the 5% paths is closer to 5% than in REPS. If valid / B is close to 1 at large N, the stock-coverage part of the explanation of the growing gap is wrong and is removed from this README and the page.

## DPS arm (distribution packet spraying, `-load_balancing_algo dps`; started 2026-11-10)
Mechanism and verification: `../../MODIFICATIONS.md` section `[ADDED: dps]`, `../../DPS_ARCHITECTURE.md`, `data/dps_smoke.log` (51 PASS). DPS = every packet draws its EV independently from a fixed distribution; no buffer, no feedback, so B plays no role (one cell per latency and seed, B = 8 is passed and ignored). Question it answers: how much of god mode's gain is the knowledge of the right distribution, and how much is the REPS buffer.

Files: `scripts/make_knee16_dps_configs.py` -> `data/configs_knee16_dps.json`, `data/bsmap_knee16_dps.json` (cfgs `h512_p16_msgNhDcap_lat<ns>`, derived from the god cfg: same fabric, workload, degrade file, 64 traced flows); `scripts/run_dps16.sh` (3 seeds (42-44, enough for this arm), 7 latencies, 1 distribution = 21 cells). `scripts/run_knee16.py` was edited (not new-files-only, but the same pattern as its existing `configs_knee16_q.json` hook): when a cfg has a `dps` field the `-load_balancing_algo freezing` of `run_m4.flags` is replaced by `dps` and `-dps_dist <args>` is appended. Container layout: `scripts/setup_dps_container.sh`.

Arm run here: **`cap` = `-dps_dist capacity` only, the 95% / 5% case** (weights 20:380 = god-mode weights without the buffer: 5% of the packets on the 8 slow links, 95% on the 8 fast ones). The mechanism supports any distribution (`DPS_ARCHITECTURE.md`), but by decision only this one is run in this experiment. A first launch also included `uniform`, `zipf 1.0` and `normal 8 4`; it was stopped after 34 cells (lat 250, seeds 42-44 and part of lat 500), and those cells are not used (moved aside in the container, `/k16_dps_dropped`).
