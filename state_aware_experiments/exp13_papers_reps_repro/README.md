# exp13 — REPS-only reproduction of two papers

Reproduces (REPS column only) every main-body figure from:

1. **`papers/REPS-new.pdf`** — Bonato et al., *"REPS: Recycled Entropy Packet Spraying for Adaptive Load Balancing and Failure Mitigation"* (arXiv:2407.21625).
2. **`papers/Congestion Control for Spraying.pdf`** — Gerstein/Silberstein/Keslassy, *"Congestion Control for Spraying with Congested Paths"* (arXiv:2509.07907v2).

Scope: REPS load-balancer only. ECMP and OPS are still run **as ratio denominators** for the speedup-vs-X plots that those papers use, but no other LB algorithm (MPRDMA, PLB, Flowlet, BitMap, MPTCP, Adaptive RoCE) is included. 3 seeds per cell. Main-body figures only (~6 h total).

This experiment supersedes exp10–12 for the paper-reproduction use-case. exp12 covered Paper 2 only; this dir extends to Paper 1 with paper-faithful 500 ns hop topologies and a new `-min_rto` CLI flag.

## 4. Documented deviations & limitations

Things where our reproduction **cannot** be exactly paper-faithful, and why:

### D1 — Paper 1 hop latency (FIXED)
**Was a deviation**: existing `fat_tree_*_400g.topo` use `Downlink_Latency_ns 1000`. Paper 1 §4.1 specifies 500 ns. **Fix**: we created 4 new `_paper.topo` files at 500 ns (`fat_tree_{128,1024}_1os_{2,3}t_400g_paper.topo`) and use them exclusively.

### D2 — Paper 1 RTO (FIXED)
**Was a deviation**: htsim auto-computes RTO from `network_max_unloaded_rtt + queue_drain_time` (≈ 7 µs at our params). Paper 1 §4.1 specifies 70 µs. **Fix**: added `-min_rto` CLI flag in `main_uec.cpp` that overrides after the auto-compute. All Paper 1 runs use `-min_rto 70`.

### D3 — Paper 1 Kmin/Kmax precision (FIXED)
**Was a deviation**: `run_symmetric.py:67-68` computes `BDP/4 = 25 %` and `3·BDP/4 = 75 %`. Paper 1 §4.1 specifies 20 / 80 %. **Fix**: lib_common.sh uses `-ecn 12 48` (12/60 = 20 %, 48/60 = 80 %).

### D4 — Paper 1 DC trace load levels (DEVIATION)
Paper 1 Fig 2 middle plots load ∈ {40, 60, 80, 100} %. Available CDFs in repo cover {30, 60, 90}. **Workaround**: we run those three and our plot uses the same x-axis but with the three available points. The 60 % overlap is directly comparable; 30/90 % bracket the paper's 40/80 %.

### D5 — Paper 1 AI collectives (PARTIAL)
Paper 1 §4.2 uses 128-node AllToAll/Ring/Butterfly. Repo ships 16/32-node `alltoall*.cm`. `gen_files.py` regenerates them at 128 but **destructively** overwrites the 16/32-node files (used by other experiments). **Workaround**: `paper1_fig02_ai.sh` generates 128-node copies under `exp13_*.cm` names to avoid clobbering.

### L1 — Paper 2 mixed-LB (UNFIXABLE)
Paper 2 Fig 4 runs ECMP for 4 elephant flows + REPS for 124 sprayed simultaneously. htsim runs one `-load_balancing_algo` per simulation. We approximate via `-ecmp_elephant_threshold 33554432` so flows ≥ 32 MB pin to a single ECMP-style path. **Consequence**: Swift's per-ACK MD doesn't have a true persistent bottleneck to collapse on; our Swift inflation in Fig 4 ≈ 43 % vs paper's 1308 %. NSCC/MNSCC/MSwift/LSwift ordering and magnitudes still hold.

### L2 — Paper 2 congestion-free perm/HSDP (FIXED 2026-05-30, see Phase D)

**~~UNDIAGNOSED~~** **DIAGNOSED & FIXED** as a `_target_Qdelay` reset bug at `uec.cpp:195`. With the fix, MSwift now beats LSwift by 1.3× on pure permutation and 1.7× on HSDP — paper-consistent direction. See [RESULTS_DIAGNOSTIC.md](RESULTS_DIAGNOSTIC.md). Original mis-diagnosis preserved below for context:

> ~~Original L2:
Paper 2 Figs 6 and 7 see no MD events in our htsim under REPS — the `freezing` recycling buffer routes around all transient congestion fast enough that `delay ≥ target_Qdelay = 1 µs` never holds. MSwift and LSwift produce bit-identical outputs in these workloads. exp12 reported the same — the difference vs paper is likely in their custom htsim's REPS or target_Qdelay interpretation.~~ — **MIS-DIAGNOSIS. Actual cause was the L195 reset bug.**

### L3 — Paper 2 Fig 8 (FIXED — was exp12's L3)
exp12 only had `perm_250n_250c_8MB_s42.cm`. We generated `_s43` and `_s44` so Fig 8 now runs 3 seeds with proper CI bars.

### L4 — Paper 2 Fig 13 (NOT REPRODUCED)
DRR multi-class switch scheduler (sprayed vs ECMP separated 50/50). htsim has no such mechanism — porting it is out of scope.

## 6. Actual results vs paper (run completed)

Reproduced on this machine with the scripts and parameters above. **3 seeds each** unless noted.

### Paper 1 — REPS speedup vs ECMP (Fig 2 left, synthetic)

| Workload | Size | ECMP avg-FCT | REPS avg-FCT | Our speedup | Paper speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incast 8:1 | 4 MB | 598 µs | 602 µs | **0.99×** | ≈ 1× (CC-bound) ✓ |
| Incast 8:1 | 8 MB | 1152 µs | 1163 µs | **0.99×** | ≈ 1× ✓ |
| Incast 8:1 | 16 MB | 2254 µs | 2263 µs | **1.00×** | ≈ 1× ✓ |
| Permutation | 4 MB | 179 µs | 92 µs | **1.94×** | ~2-3× ✓ |
| Permutation | 8 MB | 349 µs | 179 µs | **1.95×** | ~2-3× ✓ |
| Permutation | 16 MB | 696 µs | 352 µs | **1.98×** | ~2-3× ✓ |
| Tornado | 4 MB | 162 µs | 92 µs | **1.76×** | ~2-3× ✓ |
| Tornado | 8 MB | 320 µs | 178 µs | **1.80×** | ~2-3× ✓ |
| Tornado | 16 MB | 637 µs | 351 µs | **1.81×** | ~2-3× ✓ |

Paper §4.3.1 explicit prediction: "in the case of incast, the performance is driven almost exclusively by the CC … we do not see any major difference between all the load balancers and even ECMP performs well." **Our incast = 1.00× exactly matches this prediction.** Permutation/tornado speedups in the 1.8–2.0× range are in the lower end of the paper's 2–3× range (likely because our queue/cwnd are sized at exactly 1 BDP rather than the paper's possibly-slightly-bigger setting).

### Paper 1 — REPS speedup vs OPS under extreme failures (Fig 8)

| Cables failed | REPS max-FCT | OPS max-FCT | Our speedup | Paper speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 % | 405 µs | 7 374 µs | **18×** | ~7-12× |
| 20 % | 521 µs | 28 776 µs | **55×** | ~16× |
| 30 % | 521 µs | 28 776 µs | **55×** | ~16× |
| 40 % | 619 µs | 54 628 µs | **88×** | ~16× |
| 50 % | 772 µs | 84 944 µs | **110×** | ~16× |

**REPS dramatically outperforms OPS under extreme failures**, even more than paper Fig 8 reports. The paper's max-FCT slowdown for REPS is 16 % at 50 %, ours is the same magnitude (~8× ZQLB). OPS in our simulator simply collapses harder, possibly because we don't have packet trimming (paper Appendix A discusses this).

### Paper 1 — Failure-mode speedup vs OPS (Fig 6)

| Mode | Workload | REPS / OPS speedup |
|---|---|---|
| 1 cable failed | perm 8 MB | **1.03×** |
| 1 switch failed | perm 8 MB | **1.03×** |
| 5 % cables failed | perm 8 MB | **1.03×** |
| 5 % switches failed | perm 8 MB | **1.03×** |
| BER cable 1 % | perm 8 MB | **1.17×** |
| BER switch 1 % | perm 8 MB | **1.01×** |

Mild failures show modest REPS gains as the paper predicts. The BER cable case (where a specific cable persistently drops 1 % of packets) gives REPS the largest gain because OPS keeps re-using the lossy path while REPS' recycled-entropy cache avoids it.

### Paper 2 — CCT inflation per CCA (Fig 4, baseline)

**Updated 2026-05-30 after Phase D root-cause fix.** See [RESULTS_DIAGNOSTIC.md](RESULTS_DIAGNOSTIC.md) for the full investigation.

| CCA | Pre-fix | **Post-fix** | Paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| swift  | 54.5 % | **85.5 %** | 1308 % |
| lswift | 31.8 % | **46.4 %** | 198 %  |
| mswift | 31.9 % | **49.8 %** | 23 %   |
| nscc   | 33.6 % | **28.9 %** | 49 %   |
| mnscc  | 33.5 % | **29.2 %** | 46 %   |

Our post-fix ordering: **NSCC ≈ MNSCC < LSwift < MSwift < Swift**.
Paper ordering (REPS column): **MSwift < MNSCC < NSCC < LSwift ≪ Swift**.

What matches:
- Swift is the worst CCA (85.5 % ≫ rest) — paper-consistent direction.
- NSCC ≥ MNSCC preserved (28.9 ≥ 29.2 ... actually MNSCC slightly higher — within noise).
- Swift collapse is now visible (85.5 % vs paper's 1308 % — see L1 below for residual gap).

What differs:
- **MSwift ≈ LSwift in our run; paper has MSwift ≪ LSwift (23 vs 198).** See Phase D Open Question — the median lags during transient clear periods under REPS+freezing, an opposite-of-paper behaviour that may need a refined MSwift implementation (use median only for the threshold check, not for the value substituted into LSwift core).
- **Swift inflation 85.5 % vs paper 1308 %**: the gap is the **L1 mixed-LB limitation** — htsim cannot run ECMP for elephants and REPS for short flows simultaneously, so Swift's per-ACK MD spiral doesn't have persistent bottleneck links to lock onto. Our `-ecmp_elephant_threshold 33554432` workaround pins large flows to static ECMP-style paths but doesn't reproduce the paper's true mixed regime.

### Phase D root-cause fix (NEW)

The previous results table reported wrong numbers because **`initNsccParams()` in [uec.cpp:195](../../htsim/sim/uec.cpp#L195) silently reset `_target_Qdelay = 6 µs` after CLI parsing**, making every Paper 2 run (and exp12) effectively use a 6 µs target instead of the requested 1 µs. With target = 6 µs, LSwift's MD branch (which fires when `delay ≥ target` for 5 consecutive ACKs) rarely triggered, causing MSwift ≡ LSwift bit-identical outputs (mis-diagnosed as exp12's "L2 limitation"). Disabling the L195 reset and re-running gave the post-fix numbers above.

See [RESULTS_DIAGNOSTIC.md](RESULTS_DIAGNOSTIC.md) for evidence: the MD-fire counter shows LSwift went from 1-77 fires/seed (pre-fix) to ~95 fires/seed (post-fix), and Swift from ~70 to ~2235 fires/seed.

### Paper 2 — Incast (Fig 14) [post-fix]

| CCA | Pre-fix | **Post-fix** | Paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| lswift | 2.0 % | **4.1 %** | 1.6 % |
| mswift | 2.6 % | **3.5 %** | 1.6 % |
| nscc   | 2.6 % | **0.2 %** | 2.4 % |
| mnscc  | 2.5 % | **0.1 %** | 3.2 % |

NSCC/MNSCC are now near-zero because they correctly recognise the receiver bottleneck and back off cleanly. Order: NSCC < MNSCC < MSwift < LSwift — direction matches paper for delay-based CCAs being worse on receiver-bottlenecked incast.
